4.1 · Harder task

A biased sample with correct totals

A population has 600 commuters and 400 non-commuters. A sample of 80 should be proportionally stratified. A researcher instead takes volunteers within each quota.

Part 1

Hint

Apply 600/1000 to the sample size.

Worked solution

0.6×80=48 commuters.

Part 2

Hint

Use 400/1000 of 80.

Worked solution

0.4×80=32 non-commuters.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

Within each stratum, selection must be random. Volunteers can differ systematically in interest, availability or travel habits, so quota proportions do not eliminate selection bias.

Estimate a grouped percentile

Equal-width intervals [0,10), [10,20), [20,30), [30,40) have frequencies 6,14,16,4. Use linear interpolation within classes.

Summarizing data

Use frequencies as weights.

Part 1

Hint

Locate cumulative position 0.5×40.

Worked solution

Cumulative counts are 6,20,36,40. Position 20 is at the boundary 20.

Part 2

Hint

Locate cumulative position 0.85×40 and interpolate within its class.

Worked solution

Position 34 lies in [20,30), where cumulative frequency rises from 20 to 36. Estimate = 20 + (34−20)×10/16 = 28.75.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

No. Class frequencies lose individual values. A cumulative graph provides estimated percentiles, but exact quartiles and individual outlier labels require the raw observations and a stated quartile convention.

4.3 · Harder task

Transform a weighted mean

Group A has 8 observations with mean 12. Group B has 12 observations with mean 17. The combined population SD is 4. Transform every value by y=3x−5.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Summarizing data

IQR = Q₃ − Q₁; mean = Σ(fx)/Σf

Use frequencies as weights.

Part 1

Hint

Combine sums, not the two unweighted means.

Worked solution

(8×12+12×17)/20=15.

Part 2

Hint

SD is multiplied by 3; variance by 9.

Worked solution

Original variance=4²=16; transformed variance=9×16=144.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

New mean=3×15−5=40. Translation moves all values and their mean equally, leaving distances from the mean unchanged.

4.4 · Harder task

Residuals and extrapolation

A regression model is y=1.8x+4, fitted using x-values from 2 to 8. At x=5 the observation is y=11.

Part 1

Hint

Use observed minus fitted.

Worked solution

Fitted y=13; residual=11−13=−2.

Part 2

Hint

Substitute 12 into the fitted line.

Worked solution

1.8×12+4=25.6.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

x=12 lies outside the observed range, so this is extrapolation. A strong association over 2≤x≤8 does not show that the same linear trend continues to 12 or establishes causation.

4.5 · Harder task

Use a complement carefully

Two fair six-sided dice are rolled. Ordered pairs are equally likely.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Event relationships

P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B); P(A|B) = P(A ∩ B)/P(B)

Check independence before multiplying.

Part 1

Hint

Complement: neither die is 6.

Worked solution

1−(5/6)²=11/36≈0.305556.

Part 2

Hint

Multiply number of experiments by the event probability.

Worked solution

720×11/36=220.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

The outcome (6,6) is counted twice. Subtract the overlap 1/36, or use the complement. Expected count is not a guaranteed actual count.

4.6 · Harder task

A diagnostic probability

A fictional test has positive probability 0.9 for affected people and 0.1 for unaffected people. Prevalence is 0.02.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Event relationships

P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B); P(A|B) = P(A ∩ B)/P(B)

Check independence before multiplying.

Part 1

Hint

Split into affected and unaffected branches.

Worked solution

0.02×0.9+0.98×0.1=0.116.

Part 2

Hint

Divide the affected-and-positive probability by all positives.

Worked solution

0.018/0.116≈0.155172.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

Sensitivity conditions on affected status; the question conditions on a positive result. At low prevalence, many positives come from the much larger unaffected group. This is a fictional probability model, not medical advice.

Hint

Set expected net gain E(X−f) to zero.

Worked solution

E(X)=0×0.6+5×0.3+20×0.1=3.5, so f=3.5.

Hint

Use linearity of expectation.

4.8 · Harder task

A binomial threshold

X is the number of successes in 8 independent trials, each with success probability 0.25.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Discrete distributions

E(X) = ΣxP(X = x); binomial mean = np, variance = np(1 − p)

For the binomial model, n counts trials and p is success probability. Trials must be independent with the same p.

Part 1

Hint

Subtract probabilities of zero and one success from 1.

Worked solution

1−0.75⁸−8(0.25)(0.75⁷)≈0.632919.

Part 2

Hint

Use np(1−p).

Worked solution

8×0.25×0.75=1.5.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

The probability of success changes as items are removed and outcomes become dependent. A binomial model requires constant p and independent trials.

4.9 · Harder task

Recover a normal model

A normal variable has 10th percentile 42 and 90th percentile 58. Use z₀.₉=1.2815515655.

Part 1

Hint

The percentiles are symmetric about the mean.

Worked solution

Mean=(42+58)/2=50.

Part 2

Hint

The distance from mean to the 90th percentile is zσ.

Worked solution

σ=8/1.2815515655≈6.24243.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

Other distributions can share these percentiles. The calculation assumes normality; assess shape, tails and context before accepting that model.

4.10 · Harder task

Tied ranks and an extreme value

x=(1,2,3,4,5), y=(10,20,20,40,100). Assign increasing averaged ranks.

Part 1

Hint

Average occupied positions 2 and 3.

Worked solution

The tied values share rank (2+3)/2=2.5.

Part 2

Hint

Compute Pearson correlation of x-ranks and y-ranks.

Worked solution

Ranks are (1,2,3,4,5) and (1,2.5,2.5,4,5). Cross-deviation sum=9.5; squared sums=10 and 9.5; rₛ=9.5/√95≈0.974679.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

The ranks do not change, so Spearman stays the same. Pearson uses numerical distances and can change substantially. Avoid the no-ties shortcut formula when ties exist.

4.11 · Harder task

A goodness-of-fit decision

A fair three-category spinner predicts equal proportions. In 60 trials the observed counts are 30,20,10. For df=2, upper-tail p=exp(−χ²/2).

Part 1

Hint

Expected counts are 20 each; sum (O−E)²/E.

Worked solution

100/20+0/20+100/20=10; df=3−1=2.

Part 2

Hint

Use the supplied df=2 survival formula.

Worked solution

p=e⁻⁵≈0.00673795.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

Reject equal probabilities at both levels because p<0.01. This does not prove a particular alternative distribution or give P(H₀ true). Independent trials and adequate expected counts are required; all E=20>5.
