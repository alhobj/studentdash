5.1 · Harder task

From a secant to an instantaneous rate

Distance is s(t)=t²+3t metres. Compare rates at t=2 seconds.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Power derivatives

d(xⁿ)/dx = nxⁿ⁻¹

Differentiate each term.

Part 1

Skills: Arithmetic and order of operations

Hint

Use [s(2.5)−s(2)]/0.5.

Worked solution

s(2)=10; s(2.5)=13.75; average=3.75/0.5=7.5.

Part 2

Skills: Arithmetic and order of operations

Hint

Differentiate or take the limit of the secant expression.

Worked solution

s′(t)=2t+3, so s′(2)=7.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

The quotient is [(2+h)²+3(2+h)−10]/h = 7+h for h≠0. As h approaches zero, this approaches 7.

Interpret derivative signs

f(x)=x³−6x²+9x+2 on 0≤x≤5.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Power derivatives

d(xⁿ)/dx = nxⁿ⁻¹

Differentiate each term.

Part 1

Skills: Arithmetic and order of operations

Hint

Factor f′(x)=3x²−12x+9.

Worked solution

f′=3(x−1)(x−3), so the smaller root is 1.

Part 2

Skills: Arithmetic and order of operations

Hint

The derivative is negative between its roots.

Worked solution

Decreasing for 1<x<3, an interval of length 2.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

The value measures height; the derivative measures change. For example f(2)=4>0 but f′(2)=−3<0: the graph is above the axis while falling.

Multiply the derivative equation by x³.

The original function excludes zero and is restricted to x>0. Thus x³ is nonzero, so multiplying preserves solutions within that domain; do not introduce x=0.

Intersect a normal with an axis

For y=x²−2x, find the normal at x=3.

Power derivatives

Differentiate each term.

Straight lines

m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁); y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)

Vertical lines need separate treatment.

Practise: Use a derivative at a point

Part 1

Skills: Arithmetic and order of operations

Hint

Tangent slope is 2x−2.

Worked solution

At x=3 tangent slope=4, so normal slope=−1/4.

Part 2

Skills: Arithmetic and order of operations

Hint

The point is (3,3); set y=0 in y−3=−(x−3)/4.

Worked solution

−3=−(x−3)/4 gives x−3=12, hence x=15.

Recover a function and an area

F′(x)=3x²−4x+5 and F(1)=7. The integrand 3x²−4x+5 is positive.

Antiderivatives and area

∫xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n + 1) + C, n ≠ −1

Increase the power and divide by the new power; this rule excludes n = −1. Use a boundary condition to find C, or evaluate between limits for a definite integral.

Part 1

Skills: Arithmetic and order of operations

Hint

Apply the boundary value at x=1.

Worked solution

7=1−2+5+C=4+C, so C=3.

Part 2

Skills: Arithmetic and order of operations

Hint

Evaluate the antiderivative at both limits.

Worked solution

F(3)−F(1)=(27−18+15+3)−7=20.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

The boundary condition fixes a particular antiderivative. In F(3)−F(1), the same C is subtracted from itself, so it cancels.

Skills: Arithmetic and order of operations

Check endpoints as well as stationary points.

Worked solution

It is greater than nearby values but not all values in the domain. The endpoint at x=3 has a much larger value; closed-interval optimization must inspect endpoints.

Areas and volumes

Keep length units consistent.

Part 1

Skills: Arithmetic and order of operations

Hint

V=x(20−2x)(12−2x); solve V′=0 and keep roots in the domain.

Worked solution

V′=240−128x+12x². The allowed root is (16−√76)/3≈2.42740.

Part 2

Skills: Arithmetic and order of operations

Hint

Substitute the unrounded x into the volume expression.

Worked solution

V≈262.682 cm³. The other stationary root is outside the physical domain.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

V′ changes positive to negative at the allowed root. Volume tends to zero at both ends of (0,6), so this stationary point gives the global physical maximum.
