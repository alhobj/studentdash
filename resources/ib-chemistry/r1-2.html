Reshape an energy profile

Move the product energy and the forward activation barrier. Toggle a catalyst to compare paths with the same reactants and products.

Your challenge: Create an exothermic profile, then make it endothermic. Switch on the catalyst: which energies change and which remain unchanged?

Model, method and limits

Reactant energy is the reference zero. ΔH = Eproducts − Ereactants. Forward and reverse activation energies are measured from their respective starting levels to the peak. The catalyst lowers both barriers equally while keeping the peak above both endpoints. The reaction coordinate is not time.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Add two steps

For fictional states A, B and C: A → B has ΔH = +45 kJ mol⁻¹ and B → C has ΔH = −120 kJ mol⁻¹. Find ΔH for A → C.

Answer: -75. Add the steps: +45 + (−120) = −75 kJ mol⁻¹. B cancels from the overall change.

Reverse a step

A → C has ΔH = −75 kJ mol⁻¹. Find ΔH for C → A.

Answer: 75. Reversing the chemical change reverses the enthalpy sign: +75 kJ mol⁻¹.

Bond enthalpy estimate

For one mole of a fictional gas-phase reaction, breaking bonds requires 680 kJ and forming bonds releases 850 kJ. Estimate ΔH.

Answer: -170. ΔH ≈ sum of bond enthalpies broken − sum formed = 680 − 850 = −170 kJ mol⁻¹. Bond breaking requires energy; bond formation releases energy.

Calculate from formation enthalpies

Use ΔH°reaction = ΣνΔH°f(products) − ΣνΔH°f(reactants). All supplied values for the fictional substances are in kJ mol⁻¹.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

One-to-one reaction

For A → B, ΔH°f(A) = −80 and ΔH°f(B) = −140. Calculate ΔH°reaction.

Answer: -60. Products minus reactants: −140 − (−80) = −60 kJ mol⁻¹ for the reaction as written.

Use the coefficients

For 2A → B, ΔH°f(A) = −80 and ΔH°f(B) = −140. Calculate ΔH°reaction for the equation as written.

Answer: 20. −140 − 2(−80) = +20 kJ mol⁻¹. Multiply each formation enthalpy by its stoichiometric coefficient.

Reference state

What is the standard enthalpy of formation of an element in its standard state?

Answer: 0. It is zero by convention. This does not mean every compound has zero formation enthalpy, or that all physical forms of an element have zero values.
