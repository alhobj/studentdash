Mix an isotope sample

Move the abundance slider to blend two fictional isotopes with relative masses 20.0 and 22.0. Watch both the atom sample and weighted mean change.

Your challenge: Make a relative atomic mass of 21.20, then check your mixture. Predict which isotope should be more abundant before moving the slider.

Model, method and limits

Aᵣ = 20.0(1 − f) + 22.0f, where f is the fraction of the heavier isotope. Aᵣ = 21.20 needs 60% of the heavier isotope. The 20 displayed atoms are a rounded illustration; the calculation uses the slider percentage exactly.

S1.2 / Practice

Read an atom or ion

Select a species. Predict its proton, neutron and electron counts, then reveal the nuclear model. Mass number A is a whole-number nucleon count, not relative atomic mass.

How to find the counts

Protons = Z. Neutrons = A − Z. Electrons = Z − charge, where the charge is signed. For ²³Na⁺, Z = 11 and A = 23: 11 protons, 12 neutrons and 10 electrons.

Protons (+1) and neutrons (0) are in a tiny, dense nucleus containing almost all the atom's mass. Electrons (−1) occupy the surrounding region and have much smaller mass. The atom is mostly empty space; any schematic nucleus is enlarged for readability.

Isotopes have the same proton number but different neutron numbers. Forming an ion changes the electron count, not the nucleus.

Find relative atomic mass

Model a hypothetical sample containing only carbon-12 and carbon-13. Use 12 and 13 as approximate isotope masses. Change the abundance and predict the weighted mean. These slider values are not a claim about natural abundance.

Relative atomic mass is dimensionless. An average such as 12.25 does not mean that an individual nucleus contains a fractional neutron.

Work backwards from an isotope average

Use a fictional element X with only two isotopes, of relative isotopic masses 20.0 and 22.0. Relative atomic mass is a weighted mean, not a mass number.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Weighted mean

X-20 has abundance 75.0% and X-22 has abundance 25.0%. Find the relative atomic mass.

Answer: 20.5. 20.0 × 0.750 + 22.0 × 0.250 = 20.5. The more abundant isotope contributes more to the average.

Find an abundance

Another sample has relative atomic mass 21.2. What percentage of its atoms are X-22?

Answer: 60. Let f be the fraction of X-22. 20(1 − f) + 22f = 21.2, giving f = 0.600, or 60.0%.

Check a claim

A sample containing only these two isotopes is reported to have relative atomic mass 23.0. Is this possible?

Answer: No, the mean must lie between 20.0 and 22.0. A weighted mean with non-negative abundances cannot lie outside the range of its constituent values.
