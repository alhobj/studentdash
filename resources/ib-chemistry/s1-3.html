Trigger an electron transition

Choose an energy level to move the electron. Watch the atom absorb or emit a photon whose energy matches the gap.

Your challenge: Compare a direct drop from level 4 to level 1 with a sequence through the intermediate levels. Is the total emitted energy the same? Are the photons the same?

Model, method and limits

The fictional bound levels are 0, 2.0, 3.5 and 4.2 eV relative to the ground state. Photon energy equals the level difference. The approximate conversion wavelength / nm = 1240 / (energy / eV) is supplied for exploration, not required memorization. Transitions are deliberately triggered, not a prediction of lifetimes or selection rules.

S1.3 / Practice

Fill subshells and orbitals

Practise selected ground-state atoms and ions, including chromium and copper. Enter subshell notation with spaces, for example 1s2 2s2 2p6 or [Ne]. Carets and superscript digits are also accepted. The checker accepts different subshell ordering, but requires the correct occupancies.

Boxes represent orbitals, not paths through space. Arrows represent opposite spin states. Diagram order is a filling guide, not a quantitative energy scale.

Rules and common traps
  • An orbital holds at most two electrons with opposite spins (Pauli exclusion principle).
  • Equal-energy orbitals fill singly with parallel spins before pairing (Hund's rule). An s subshell has 1 orbital, p has 3 and d has 5.
  • A useful initial filling order is 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d. For the selected neutral atoms, remember Cr is [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹ and Cu is [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹.
  • For the transition-metal cations here, remove 4s electrons before 3d electrons. Fe²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁶; Fe³⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁵.

Check the electron total against atomic number and charge. The same electron count alone does not prove a correct configuration.

Explain: Compare the unpaired electrons of N and O, then compare Fe, Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺. Why can simply filling orbitals for the ion's electron count give a wrong transition-metal configuration?

Read an electron transition

Connect discrete energy levels with line emission spectra. These questions concern photons and transitions rather than filling orbitals.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Emit or absorb?

An electron moves from a higher energy level to a lower energy level. What happens?

Answer: A photon is emitted. The atom emits a photon with energy equal to the difference between the two levels.

Compare photons

Transition A has a larger energy gap than transition B. How does the emitted photon from A compare?

Answer: Higher frequency and shorter wavelength. E = hf and c = fλ. Greater photon energy means greater frequency and shorter wavelength; the speed in vacuum is unchanged.

Why lines?

Why does an isolated atomic gas produce discrete spectral lines rather than every possible wavelength?

Answer: Only specific electron energy differences are allowed. Quantized energy levels allow only particular transition energies, producing characteristic wavelengths.
