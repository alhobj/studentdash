Run a virtual calorimeter

Set a water mass and the fraction of supplied heat retained. Press the heater button to add a known energy pulse and record the temperature response.

Your challenge: With no heat loss, compare the temperature rise for 100 g and 200 g after the same energy pulse. Then introduce heat loss and explain why the measured rise is smaller.

Model, method and limits

ΔT = qretained / (mc), using c = 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹ and an initial temperature of 20 °C. Container heat capacity and evaporation are omitted. Changing the setup clears the run; heat is added only when the heater button is pressed.

Follow the heat in a calorimeter

Use fictional measurements and q = mcΔT. Assume no heat loss and neglect the heat capacity of the container. The solution and reaction have opposite heat signs.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Heat gained by solution

100.0 g of solution warms by 5.00 K. Use c = 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹. Calculate the heat gained by the solution in kJ.

Answer: 2.09. q = 100.0 × 4.18 × 5.00 = 2090 J = +2.09 kJ. The reaction releases this heat.

Molar reaction enthalpy

A reaction involving 0.0500 mol of the limiting reactant transfers 2.09 kJ to its surroundings. Find ΔH per mole of that reactant.

Answer: -41.8. q(reaction) = −2.09 kJ, so ΔH = −2.09 / 0.0500 = −41.8 kJ mol⁻¹. Heat release is exothermic.

Cooling solution

The solution cools during a reaction in an insulated calorimeter. What is the sign of the reaction enthalpy?

Answer: Positive: endothermic. The reaction absorbs heat from the solution. The solution loses thermal energy while the reacting system gains it.

Diagnose a calorimetry result

Distinguish heat transfer, molar enthalpy and systematic heat loss using fictional calorimeter measurements.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Heat loss

An exothermic reaction loses heat to the room, but the calculation uses only the solution's temperature rise. How does the calculated ΔH compare with the true value?

Answer: Less negative. The measured temperature rise is too small, so the calculated heat released is too small in magnitude. The result is closer to zero.

Include the container

The solution absorbs 2.00 kJ and the calorimeter absorbs 0.30 kJ. Assume no other heat transfer. Find q for the reaction.

Answer: -2.3. q(reaction) = −[q(solution) + q(calorimeter)] = −(2.00 + 0.30) = −2.30 kJ.

Scale the reaction

Twice as much reactant undergoes the same reaction under the same conditions. What happens to the molar enthalpy change?

Answer: It stays the same. Total heat doubles, but dividing by twice the reacted amount gives the same enthalpy change per mole.
