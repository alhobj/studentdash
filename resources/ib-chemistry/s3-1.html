S3.1 / Practice

Explain a periodic pattern

Compare positions and electron arrangements. Use shielding, occupied shells and nuclear charge to explain a trend rather than memorizing an arrow.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Across period 3

Which atom is generally smaller: sodium or chlorine?

Answer: Chlorine. Across period 3 the nuclear charge increases while electrons are added to the same main shell. Similar shielding and greater attraction generally decrease atomic radius.

Down group 1

Which requires less energy to remove its first electron as a gaseous atom: lithium or potassium?

Answer: Potassium. Potassium's outer electron is in a more distant shell and is more shielded. These effects outweigh its larger nuclear charge.

Read the arrangement

A neutral main-group atom has electron arrangement 2,8,7. In which group and period is it found?

Answer: Group 17, period 3. Three occupied main shells indicate period 3. Seven outer-shell electrons place this main-group element in group 17.

S3.1 / Practice

Predict oxide behaviour

Connect metallic and non-metallic character to acid–base behaviour of period 3 oxides. Aluminium oxide provides an important exception to a simple two-way classification.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Sodium oxide

Na₂O reacts with water to form NaOH. How is this oxide classified?

Answer: Basic. Sodium oxide is a basic oxide. Na₂O + H₂O → 2NaOH gives an alkaline solution.

Sulfur trioxide

SO₃ reacts with water to form sulfuric acid. How is this oxide classified?

Answer: Acidic. Sulfur trioxide is an acidic non-metal oxide. SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄.

Aluminium oxide

Al₂O₃ reacts with both acids and bases. Which term describes this behaviour?

Answer: Amphoteric. An amphoteric oxide can react with either acids or bases. This does not mean that it dissolves readily in water.
