R3.4 / Practice

Follow the donated electron pair

Recognize electron-pair donors and acceptors and predict simple substitution and addition products. Distinguish a full electron pair from the single electrons in radical reactions.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Nucleophile

In CH₃Br + OH⁻ → CH₃OH + Br⁻, which species donates the electron pair that forms the new C–O bond?

Answer: OH⁻. Hydroxide donates a lone pair to the electrophilic carbon. The C–Br bond breaks and bromide leaves.

Substitution product

Bromoethane reacts with aqueous hydroxide by substitution. What organic product forms?

Answer: Ethanol, CH₃CH₂OH. The hydroxyl group replaces bromine on the carbon chain. The stated pathway is substitution, not elimination.

Addition to ethene

Hydrogen bromide adds across the C=C bond of ethene. Which product forms?

Answer: Bromoethane, CH₃CH₂Br. H and Br add to the two carbons of the double bond. The carbon–carbon bond becomes single and no small molecule is eliminated.

Compare substitution mechanisms (AHL)

Compare idealized SN1 and SN2 mechanisms in suitable conditions. The labels describe the molecularity of the rate-determining step, not the number of products.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

SN2 concentration change

An SN2 reaction follows rate = k[halogenoalkane][nucleophile]. Both concentrations are doubled at fixed temperature. By what factor does the rate change?

Answer: 4. Doubling each of the two first-order concentration terms multiplies the rate by 2 × 2 = 4.

SN1 intermediate

Which species is the characteristic intermediate in the idealized SN1 substitution of a tertiary halogenoalkane?

Answer: A carbocation. The leaving group departs in the slow first step, forming a carbocation. The nucleophile then attacks the electron-deficient carbon.

SN2 stereochemistry

An SN2 reaction occurs at a chiral carbon. What is the characteristic stereochemical outcome at that centre?

Answer: Inversion of configuration. Backside nucleophilic attack and simultaneous leaving-group departure invert the spatial arrangement at the reacting carbon.
