3.1 · Harder task

A composite container

A cylinder of radius 3 cm and height 8 cm is capped by a hemisphere of the same radius. Include the flat circular bottom but not the internal shared face.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Areas and volumes

A(circle) = πr²; V(cylinder) = πr²h

Keep length units consistent.

Coordinate geometry

d = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²)

Average coordinates for midpoints.

Three-dimensional geometry

V(cone) = πr²h/3; V(sphere) = 4πr³/3

Distinguish height from slant length.

Part 1

Hint

Add cylinder volume and half a sphere.

Worked solution

72π + 18π = 90π cm³.

Part 2

Hint

Add curved cylinder, curved hemisphere and bottom disk.

Worked solution

48π + 18π + 9π = 75π cm².

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

Area scales by 2²=4 and volume by 2³=8. The shared internal circular face is still excluded; doubling does not change which surfaces are external.

3.2 · Harder task

Resolve an ambiguous triangle

In triangle ABC, a=7, b=10 and angle A=30°. Lower-case sides are opposite matching angles.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Triangle relationships

a/sin A = b/sin B; c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C

Match each side with its opposite angle. Check your calculator is in degrees for these SL tasks.

Part 1

Hint

Use sin B / b = sin A / a.

Worked solution

sin B=10sin30°/7=5/7, giving B≈45.5847°.

Part 2

Hint

Sine has the same value at B and 180°−B.

Worked solution

The other B≈134.415°. Both leave positive angle C.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

Compute C=180°−A−B in each case. Here C≈104.415° or 15.5847°, both positive; if one were zero or negative that candidate would be rejected.

3.3 · Harder task

Combine two journeys

A boat travels 12 km on bearing 030°, then 8 km on bearing 120°. Bearings are clockwise from north.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Triangle relationships

a/sin A = b/sin B; c² = a² + b² − 2ab cos C

Match each side with its opposite angle. Check your calculator is in degrees for these SL tasks.

Part 1

Hint

East components use distance × sin(bearing).

Worked solution

12sin30°+8sin120°=6+4√3≈12.9282 km.

Part 2

Hint

Sum north components too, then use Pythagoras.

Worked solution

North = 6√3−4≈6.3923. Distance = √208≈14.4222 km.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

The 20 km total measures the route length. Displacement is the straight vector from start to finish; the direction changes between legs.

3.4 · Harder task

An annular sector

A sector-shaped strip has outer radius 10 cm, inner radius 6 cm and angle 72°.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Arcs and sectors

arc = (θ/360)2πr; sector area = (θ/360)πr²

Use degrees for these formulas.

Practise: Choose an arc formula

Part 1

Hint

Subtract two sector areas with the same angle.

Worked solution

(72/360)π(10²−6²)=12.8π cm².

Part 2

Hint

Include both arcs and the two radial segments.

Worked solution

Outer plus inner arcs = (72/360)2π(10+6)=6.4π; radial sides total 2(10−6)=8.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

Only the strip is bounded: each straight side runs from radius 6 to radius 10 and has length 4, not 10.

3.5 · Harder task

A boundary between sites

A=(1,2), B=(7,6). Their perpendicular bisector has equation y=mx+c.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Coordinate geometry

d = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²)

Average coordinates for midpoints.

Straight lines

m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁); y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)

Vertical lines need separate treatment.

Part 1

Hint

Find AB’s slope and use its negative reciprocal.

Worked solution

AB slope=4/6=2/3, so m=−3/2.

Part 2

Hint

Use midpoint (4,4).

Worked solution

4=−1.5×4+c, giving c=10.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

It satisfies y=−1.5x+10. Squared distances to A and B are 1²+5²=26 and (−5)²+1²=26; equal distances confirm the boundary.

3.6 · Harder task

A three-site Voronoi vertex

Sites are A=(2,2), B=(8,2), C=(5,8), inside a square 0≤x,y≤10.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Coordinate geometry

d = √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²)

Average coordinates for midpoints.

Straight lines

m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁); y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)

Vertical lines need separate treatment.

Part 1

Hint

A–B boundary is x=5; equate squared distances to A and C.

Worked solution

9+(y−2)²=(y−8)² gives 12y=51, so y=4.25.

Part 2

Hint

Use the distance to C, directly above the vertex.

Worked solution

8−4.25=3.75; distance to A is √(9+2.25²)=3.75.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

No. Compare all relevant Voronoi vertices and map-boundary candidates. For example corner (0,10) is √29≈5.385 from its nearest site C, larger than 3.75.
