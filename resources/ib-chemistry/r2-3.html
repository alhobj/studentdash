R2.3 / Practice

Disturb a dynamic equilibrium

For a fictional A ⇌ B system at fixed temperature and volume, add either species and advance time. Compare the forward and reverse rates as the mixture approaches equilibrium.

Your challenge: Reach equilibrium, add more A, and watch the response. Repeat with a faster catalyst setting. Does the final ratio change?

Model, method and limits

The model uses first-order forward and reverse steps with kf = 0.20 s⁻¹ and kr = 0.05 s⁻¹, so Kc = [B]/[A] = 4. A catalyst multiplies both rate constants equally. The exact exponential relaxation conserves [A] + [B] between additions; equilibrium has equal nonzero rates.

R2.3 / Practice

Compare Q with K

H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g)

At a fixed temperature, use Qc = [HI]² / ([H₂][I₂]). Predict the direction of net reaction from the current mixture. The chosen Kc is illustrative, not a measured value at a specified temperature.

Reaction quotient

Q < KQ = KQ > K

The marker uses log₁₀(Q/K), clipped at ±2. It shows relative position, not reaction speed or an equilibrium composition.

Method and worked example

Q < K: net forward reaction increases Q. Q > K: net reverse reaction decreases Q. Q = K: the mixture is at dynamic equilibrium; both reactions continue at equal rates.

With [H₂] = [I₂] = [HI] = 1.00 mol dm⁻³, Q = 1.00. For K = 4.00, the net reaction is forward. Raising [HI] to 2.00 gives Q = 4.00, so the mixture is at equilibrium.

Changing concentrations does not change K at fixed temperature. Selecting a different K represents a different temperature scenario; this activity does not calculate the temperature dependence of K.

R2.3 / Practice

Distinguish a shift from a change in K

Consider N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), with an exothermic forward reaction. Predict the response to one changed condition at a time.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Compress the mixture

The equilibrium mixture is compressed at constant temperature. Which net change restores equilibrium?

Answer: Formation of more NH₃. The forward direction reduces gas amount from four stoichiometric moles to two. K remains unchanged at fixed temperature.

Raise temperature

For this exothermic forward reaction, what happens when temperature increases?

Answer: Equilibrium shifts left and K decreases. Heating favours the endothermic reverse direction. Temperature changes the equilibrium constant, unlike a concentration or pressure change at fixed temperature.

Add a catalyst

A catalyst is added to a mixture already at equilibrium at fixed temperature. What changes?

Answer: Both forward and reverse rates increase, but equilibrium composition is unchanged. A catalyst provides a faster pathway in both directions. It does not change the equilibrium constant or favour one equilibrium composition.
