What is the gradient of y = x² at x = 3?

Worked explanation

Area estimates

area ≈ h[y₀ + 2(y₁ + … + yₙ₋₁) + yₙ]/2

Use equally spaced strips.

Try a fixed scenario

Use one trapezoid to estimate the integral of y = x² + 1 from 0 to 4.

Worked explanation

Width 4, endpoint heights 1 and 17: area ≈ 4(1 + 17)/2 = 36. The exact value is 76/3 ≈ 25.333.

Areas and volumes

A(circle) = πr²; V(cylinder) = πr²h

Keep length units consistent.

Try a fixed scenario

What width maximizes the area when 2w + l = 40?

Worked explanation

A = w(40 − 2w), so A′ = 40 − 4w = 0 at w = 10 m. The length is 20 m and area is 200 m²; both endpoint areas are zero.

Straight lines

m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁); y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)

Vertical lines need separate treatment.

Practise: Use a derivative at a point

Try a fixed scenario

For y=x² at x=1, find the slope of the normal.

Worked explanation

Tangent slope is 2. Perpendicular slopes multiply to −1, so the normal slope is −1/2. Its equation is y−1=−0.5(x−1).

Antiderivatives and area

∫xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n + 1) + C, n ≠ −1

Increase the power and divide by the new power; this rule excludes n = −1. Use a boundary condition to find C, or evaluate between limits for a definite integral.

Try a fixed scenario

F′(x)=2x and F(1)=5. Find the constant C in F(x)=x²+C.

Worked explanation

5=1+C gives C=4. The definite integral from 1 to 3 of 2x is F(3)−F(1)=8; C cancels.

Try a fixed scenario

For f(x)=x³−3x, find the x-coordinate of the local minimum.

Worked explanation

f′=3x²−3 vanishes at ±1. Its sign changes from negative to positive at x=1, so this is a local minimum. At x=−1 it changes positive to negative, giving a local maximum.
