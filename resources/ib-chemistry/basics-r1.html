R1.1 · Start here

Measure a temperature rise

For water warming up, q = mcΔT gives energy gained by the water. In the simple insulated model, the reaction loses that energy.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Calorimetry

Q = mcΔT

Use the mass of the heated material. Convert J to kJ when needed; for an insulated setup the reaction and solution have opposite heat signs.

Constants

Nₐ ≈ 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹; R ≈ 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹

Match the required precision.

Uncertainty propagation

Choose the matching operation.

R1.2 · Start here

Read an energy change

Reaction enthalpy change = product enthalpy − reactant enthalpy. A negative value means an exothermic reaction.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Bond energies

ΔH ≈ Σ(bonds broken) − Σ(bonds formed)

Count every bond.

Thermodynamic values

Match physical states. Enthalpy values use kJ mol⁻¹, while entropy uses J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.

Combustion values

Check the product states.

Lattice energies

Values describe lattice separation.

R1.4 · AHL · Start here

Start Gibbs energy (AHL)

AHL starter: ΔG = ΔH − TΔS. Use kelvin and matching energy units. A negative ΔG indicates thermodynamic favourability under the stated conditions, not a fast reaction.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Gibbs energy (AHL)

ΔG° = ΔH° − TΔS°

Convert entropy and enthalpy to matching energy units; use kelvin. A negative result indicates thermodynamic feasibility under standard conditions.

Thermodynamic values

Match physical states. Enthalpy values use kJ mol⁻¹, while entropy uses J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.
