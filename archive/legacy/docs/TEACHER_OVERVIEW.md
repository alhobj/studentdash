# Studentdash: turn assessment feedback into a next step

A test mark tells a student how they did. Studentdash helps them understand what
to work on next—and gives you a clearer picture of where your class needs help.

Each learner gets a personal learning dashboard that brings together assessment
results, question-level feedback and practical revision suggestions. Instead of
searching through old papers, they can explore their topics and command terms,
read your advice and choose a question to practise.

For you, the class overview makes it easier to spot questions that deserve another
explanation and learners who may need a conversation. You can write feedback,
review it as the student will see it, and publish it when you are ready. Follow-up
attempts show how a learner responded to that feedback while preserving the
original assessment result.

**A simple teaching rhythm**

- **Assess:** bring together test results and the questions behind them.
- **Notice:** look for patterns across the class and within each learner’s work.
- **Respond:** give a focused comment and a manageable revision task.
- **Revisit:** use practice attempts and short exit tickets to inform the next lesson.

Exit tickets offer a quick way to check understanding. You can prepare questions,
preview them and review responses. Straightforward answers can receive immediate
marks; explanations remain yours to judge, with room for partial credit and a
personal response. Exit-ticket results stay separate from formal test results.

Students can filter their dashboard by assessment, explore the evidence behind a
percentage and tick off their own revision practice. Their individual snapshots
can also be read offline. The aim is a more useful feedback conversation, with
clearer next steps for the learner and better information for the teacher.

**Where it stands today**

Studentdash is a working prototype demonstrated with fictional learners. Its
interactive exit tickets currently support a local rehearsal, not a ready-to-use
online classroom service. Before a real pilot, the school would need to agree
how records are kept and shared, check the marking approach and try the workflow
with a small group. Suggested grades support discussion; they do not replace
your judgement or the school’s official records.

**Try it with one assessment:** review a learner’s dashboard, add one useful piece
of feedback and follow it with one short check of understanding.
