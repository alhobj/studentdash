Mix an isotope sample

Move the abundance slider to blend two fictional isotopes with relative masses 20.0 and 22.0. Watch both the atom sample and weighted mean change.

Your challenge: Make a relative atomic mass of 21.20, then check your mixture. Predict which isotope should be more abundant before moving the slider.

Model, method and limits

Aᵣ = 20.0(1 − f) + 22.0f, where f is the fraction of the heavier isotope. Aᵣ = 21.20 needs 60% of the heavier isotope. The 20 displayed atoms are a rounded illustration; the calculation uses the slider percentage exactly.

Read an atom or ion

Select a species. Predict its proton, neutron and electron counts, then reveal the nuclear model. Mass number A is a whole-number nucleon count, not relative atomic mass.

How to find the counts

Protons = Z. Neutrons = A − Z. Electrons = Z − charge, where the charge is signed. For ²³Na⁺, Z = 11 and A = 23: 11 protons, 12 neutrons and 10 electrons.

Protons (+1) and neutrons (0) are in a tiny, dense nucleus containing almost all the atom's mass. Electrons (−1) occupy the surrounding region and have much smaller mass. The atom is mostly empty space; any schematic nucleus is enlarged for readability.

Isotopes have the same proton number but different neutron numbers. Forming an ion changes the electron count, not the nucleus.

Find relative atomic mass

Model a hypothetical sample containing only carbon-12 and carbon-13. Use 12 and 13 as approximate isotope masses. Change the abundance and predict the weighted mean. These slider values are not a claim about natural abundance.

Relative atomic mass is dimensionless. An average such as 12.25 does not mean that an individual nucleus contains a fractional neutron.

Work backwards from an isotope average

Use a fictional element X with only two isotopes, of relative isotopic masses 20.0 and 22.0. Relative atomic mass is a weighted mean, not a mass number.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Weighted mean

X-20 has abundance 75.0% and X-22 has abundance 25.0%. Find the relative atomic mass.

Answer: 20.5. 20.0 × 0.750 + 22.0 × 0.250 = 20.5. The more abundant isotope contributes more to the average.

Find an abundance

Another sample has relative atomic mass 21.2. What percentage of its atoms are X-22?

Answer: 60. Let f be the fraction of X-22. 20(1 − f) + 22f = 21.2, giving f = 0.600, or 60.0%.

Check a claim

A sample containing only these two isotopes is reported to have relative atomic mass 23.0. Is this possible?

Answer: No, the mean must lie between 20.0 and 22.0. A weighted mean with non-negative abundances cannot lie outside the range of its constituent values.

Question 1

Skills: Arithmetic and algebra

A neutral atom has atomic number 17 and mass number 37. How many neutrons does it contain?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 20

The neutron number is mass number minus atomic number: 37 − 17 = 20.

Question 2

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

An ion has 13 protons, 14 neutrons and 10 electrons. What is its charge?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 3+

The charge is the number of protons minus the number of electrons: 13 − 10 = +3.

Question 3

Skills: Arithmetic and algebra

How many electrons are present in an oxide ion, O²⁻, if oxygen has atomic number 8?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 10

The ion has two more electrons than the neutral atom: 8 + 2 = 10.

Question 4

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Which pair represents isotopes of the same element?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Atoms with 6 protons and respectively 6 and 8 neutrons

Isotopes have the same proton number and different neutron numbers.

Question 5

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Which change would convert an atom of one element into an atom of a different element?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Changing the number of protons in the nucleus

The proton number defines the element; changes in electrons form ions and changes in neutrons form isotopes.

Question 6

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

An atom of calcium-44 has atomic number 20. What remains unchanged when it forms Ca²⁺?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Its numbers of protons and neutrons

Ion formation changes the number of electrons, not the composition of the nucleus.

Question 7

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Which statement correctly compares a proton with an electron?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. They have opposite charges of equal magnitude, but the proton has a much greater mass.

A proton has relative charge +1 and an electron −1; the electron's mass is very small relative to the proton's.

Question 8

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Why is most of an atom's mass concentrated in its nucleus?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The nucleus contains protons and neutrons, which are much more massive than electrons.

Protons and neutrons account for almost all atomic mass, although the nucleus occupies a very small volume.

Question 9

Skills: Arithmetic and algebra

A neutral atom contains 35 electrons and 45 neutrons. What is its mass number?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 80

A neutral atom has 35 protons, so its mass number is 35 + 45 = 80.

Question 10

Skills: Arithmetic and algebra

An ion with charge 2− contains 18 electrons and 18 neutrons. What is its atomic number?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 16

A 2− ion has two more electrons than protons, so the proton number is 18 − 2 = 16.

Question 11

Skills: Arithmetic and algebra

A hypothetical element has isotopes of mass numbers 10 and 11, with abundances of 20% and 80%. Using the mass numbers as isotopic masses, what is its relative atomic mass?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 10.8

The weighted mean is (10 × 20 + 11 × 80)/100 = 10.8.

Question 12

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

An element has only two isotopes, with masses taken as 63 and 65. Its relative atomic mass is 63.4. What is the percentage abundance of the isotope of mass 65?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 20%

If x is the fraction of the heavier isotope, 63(1 − x) + 65x = 63.4; x = 0.20.

Question 13

Skills: Arithmetic and algebra

An element has isotopes of mass numbers 24, 25 and 26 with abundances of 50%, 20% and 30%. Taking these mass numbers as isotopic masses, what is its relative atomic mass?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 24.8

The weighted mean is 24 × 0.50 + 25 × 0.20 + 26 × 0.30 = 24.8.

Question 14

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Two samples of the same element have different relative atomic masses. Which explanation can account for this?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. The samples contain different proportions of the element's isotopes.

Changing isotopic abundances changes the weighted mean atomic mass without changing the identity of the element.

Question 15

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Which statement about neutral atoms of carbon-12 and carbon-14 is correct?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. They have the same number of electrons and different numbers of neutrons.

Both isotopes have six protons and six electrons; they have six and eight neutrons respectively.

Question 16

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

A species has 9 protons, 10 neutrons and 10 electrons. Which description is correct?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A fluoride ion with mass number 19 and charge 1−

Nine protons identify fluorine, 9 + 10 = 19 gives the mass number, and the extra electron gives charge −1.

Question 17

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Atoms X and Y both have mass number 40. X has 18 protons and Y has 20 protons. Which statement is correct?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. X and Y are different elements, and X has two more neutrons than Y.

Their neutron numbers are 22 and 20. Equal mass numbers do not imply the same element.

Question 18

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Which species has the same number of electrons as a neutral neon atom (atomic number 10)?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Mg²⁺, with magnesium atomic number 12

Mg²⁺ has 12 − 2 = 10 electrons. The other species have 11, 9 and 12 respectively.

Question 19

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

An element has two naturally occurring isotopes with masses taken as 79 and 81. The isotope of mass 81 becomes more abundant in a sample. What happens to the sample's relative atomic mass?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. It increases towards 81.

Increasing the weight of the heavier isotope increases the mean, which remains between the two isotope masses.

Question 20

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

In a nuclear symbol, which quantity is given by the upper-left number, A?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. The total number of protons and neutrons

A is the mass number, also called the nucleon number; it excludes electrons.
