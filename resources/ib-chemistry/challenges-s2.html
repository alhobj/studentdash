S2.1 · Harder task

Recover an ionic formula from composition

A binary compound contains only Al and O. A 10.20 g sample contains 5.40 g Al. Use Ar(Al)=27.0 and Ar(O)=16.0.

Part 1

Hint

Find oxygen mass by difference, then divide by 16.0.

Worked solution

O mass=4.80 g; n(O)=4.80/16.0=0.300 mol.

Part 2

Hint

Compare n(Al)=5.40/27.0 with n(O).

Worked solution

Al:O=0.200:0.300=2:3, giving Al₂O₃.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

2(+3)+3(−2)=0. In the solid, ions are held at lattice positions and cannot migrate through the material; a formula ratio alone does not establish mobile charge carriers.

S2.2 · Harder task

Pairs, shape and polarity

Consider CO₂ and H₂O using simple Lewis structures. Carbon has 4 valence electrons, oxygen 6 and hydrogen 1.

Part 1

Hint

Sum one carbon and two oxygens.

Worked solution

4+2×6=16 electrons.

Part 2

Hint

After two O–H bonds use four electrons, allocate the remaining four as pairs.

Worked solution

H₂O has 8 valence electrons; two bonds use 4, leaving 4 electrons or 2 lone pairs on O.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

CO₂ is linear with two equal opposing bond dipoles, which cancel. H₂O has a bent molecular shape because of two lone pairs; its O–H dipoles do not cancel. Electron-domain arrangement and molecular shape are distinct.

Test a metallic model

Use an idealized electron-sea model. Sample A contains 0.20 mol Na atoms, each contributing one delocalized electron. Sample B contains 0.10 mol Mg atoms, each contributing two.

Part 1

Hint

Multiply atom amount by the stated contribution.

Worked solution

0.20×1=0.20 mol electrons.

Part 2

Hint

Use two electrons per Mg atom.

Worked solution

0.10×2=0.20 mol electrons.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

No. Conductivity also depends on volume, carrier mobility, temperature and scattering, none fixed here. Metallic attraction is not restricted to particular directed pairs, so layers can move while attraction between positive centres and the delocalized electrons persists.

S2.4 · Harder task

Repeat units and atom retention

An addition polymer is formed from propene, CH₂=CHCH₃. Use Ar(C)=12.0 and Ar(H)=1.0. Ignore end groups in the stated repeat-unit model.

Part 1

Hint

Addition changes the bonding but does not remove atoms from C₃H₆.

Worked solution

M(C₃H₆)=3×12+6×1=42.0 g mol⁻¹.

Part 2

Hint

Divide chain molar mass by repeat-unit molar mass.

Worked solution

21000/42=500 repeat units, ignoring end groups.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

The repeat unit is –CH₂–CH(CH₃)–, enclosed in brackets with bonds crossing them. The alkene double bond is opened to form links between units; leaving the original double bond would give incorrect carbon valence.
