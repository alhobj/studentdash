Measure a temperature rise

For water warming up, q = mcΔT gives energy gained by the water. In the simple insulated model, the reaction loses that energy.

Step 1

Give me a hint

A temperature difference has the same numerical value in °C and K.

Show the worked step

25 − 20 = 5 K.

Step 2

Give me a hint

Multiply 10 × 4.18 × 5.

Show the worked step

q = 209 J gained by the water.

R1.2 · Start here

Read an energy change

Reaction enthalpy change = product enthalpy − reactant enthalpy. A negative value means an exothermic reaction.

Step 1

Give me a hint

Subtract 80 from 30.

Show the worked step

30 − 80 = −50 kJ mol⁻¹.

Step 2

Give me a hint

A negative ΔH means energy is released to the surroundings.

Show the worked step

It is exothermic; the products are lower in enthalpy.

R1.3 · Start here

Energy per gram of fuel

Energy per gram = energy released ÷ mass of fuel. Compare like units and the same conditions.

Step 1

Give me a hint

Divide 60 by 3.

Show the worked step

60 ÷ 3 = 20 kJ g⁻¹.

Step 2

Give me a hint

Multiply 20 kJ g⁻¹ by 2 g.

Show the worked step

20 × 2 = 40 kJ.

R1.4 · AHL · Start here

Start Gibbs energy (AHL)

AHL starter: ΔG = ΔH − TΔS. Use kelvin and matching energy units. A negative ΔG indicates thermodynamic favourability under the stated conditions, not a fast reaction.

Step 1

Give me a hint

Divide by 1000.

Show the worked step

100 ÷ 1000 = 0.100 kJ mol⁻¹ K⁻¹.

Step 2

Give me a hint

Calculate 20 − (300 × 0.100).

Show the worked step

20 − 30 = −10 kJ mol⁻¹; thermodynamically favourable under these conditions.
