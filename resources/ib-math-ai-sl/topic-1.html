Build a savings sequence

Start with: SL 1.2 small steps

Change the first weekly deposit and the fixed increase. Compare the nth deposit with the total saved. This is an arithmetic sequence with no interest.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Arithmetic patterns

uₙ = u₁ + (n − 1)d; Sₙ = n(u₁ + uₙ)/2

Here d is the step between terms and n counts terms. Use uₙ for one term and Sₙ for a total.

Practise: Find a sequence term

Try a fixed scenario

With first deposit 10 and increase 5, what is the total deposited over 10 weeks?

Worked explanation

S₁₀ = 10/2 × [2(10) + 9(5)] = 325. The tenth deposit alone is 55.

Compounding

FV = PV(1 + r/(100k))ᵏⁿ

PV is the starting amount; r is the annual percentage rate, k the periods per year and n the years. This formula is for a lump sum; regular deposits or repayments need a cash-flow method or financial solver.

Practise: Use a percentage rate

Try a fixed scenario

At 5% compounded annually, what is a balance of 1000 worth after 2 years?

Worked explanation

1000 × 1.05² = 1102.50. Apply each year's increase to the updated balance.

Geometric patterns

uₙ = u₁rⁿ⁻¹; Sₙ = u₁(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r)

Here r multiplies each term to give the next. If r = 1, the total is simply n times the first term.

Try a fixed scenario

A debt of 5000 accrues 0.5% monthly interest, then a payment of 200 is made. What balance remains?

Worked explanation

5000 × 1.005 − 200 = 4825. The interest charged is 25.

Powers and logarithms

aˣ = b ⇔ x = logₐ(b)

The base a must be positive and different from 1; b must be positive.

Try a fixed scenario

Solve 10ˣ = 0.001. Find x.

Worked explanation

10⁻³ = 0.001, so log₁₀(0.001) = −3. Logarithms require positive arguments.

Try a fixed scenario

Deposit 100 at each year-end with 10% annual interest. What is the balance just after the second deposit?

Worked explanation

The first deposit grows to 110; the second deposit adds 100. Total 210, because the second deposit has not yet earned interest.

Solve a three-variable system

Start with: SL 1.8 small steps · SL 2.6 small steps

Move the three right-hand sides of x + y + z = A, x − y + z = B, and x + y − z = C. A technology-style solver finds a unique solution and displays residuals.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Straight lines

m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁); y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)

Vertical lines need separate treatment.

Quadratic symmetry

x = −b/(2a)

Identify a and b first.

Try a fixed scenario

For x+y+z=6, x−y+z=2 and x+y−z=0, find z.

Worked explanation

Subtract the third equation from the first: 2z = 6, so z = 3. Then y = 2 and x = 1.

Locate polynomial roots and intersections

Start with: SL 1.8 small steps · SL 2.4 small steps

Move q in f(x) = x³ − 3x and the horizontal line g(x) = q. The solver reports all real intersections in the displayed interval, including tangent intersections at q = ±2. Gold is g; mint is f.

Try a fixed scenario

How many distinct real solutions does x³ − 3x = 0 have?

Worked explanation

x(x² − 3) = 0 gives −√3, 0, √3. A plotted crossing is a root of f(x) − q.
