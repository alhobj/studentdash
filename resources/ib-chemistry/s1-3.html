Trigger an electron transition

Choose an energy level to move the electron. Watch the atom absorb or emit a photon whose energy matches the gap.

Your challenge: Compare a direct drop from level 4 to level 1 with a sequence through the intermediate levels. Is the total emitted energy the same? Are the photons the same?

Model, method and limits

The fictional bound levels are 0, 2.0, 3.5 and 4.2 eV relative to the ground state. Photon energy equals the level difference. The approximate conversion wavelength / nm = 1240 / (energy / eV) is supplied for exploration, not required memorization. Transitions are deliberately triggered, not a prediction of lifetimes or selection rules.

S1.3 / Practice

Fill subshells and orbitals

Practise selected ground-state atoms and ions, including chromium and copper. Enter subshell notation with spaces, for example 1s2 2s2 2p6 or [Ne]. Carets and superscript digits are also accepted. The checker accepts different subshell ordering, but requires the correct occupancies.

Boxes represent orbitals, not paths through space. Arrows represent opposite spin states. Diagram order is a filling guide, not a quantitative energy scale.

Rules and common traps
  • An orbital holds at most two electrons with opposite spins (Pauli exclusion principle).
  • Equal-energy orbitals fill singly with parallel spins before pairing (Hund's rule). An s subshell has 1 orbital, p has 3 and d has 5.
  • A useful initial filling order is 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d. For the selected neutral atoms, remember Cr is [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹ and Cu is [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹.
  • For the transition-metal cations here, remove 4s electrons before 3d electrons. Fe²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁶; Fe³⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁵.

Check the electron total against atomic number and charge. The same electron count alone does not prove a correct configuration.

Explain: Compare the unpaired electrons of N and O, then compare Fe, Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺. Why can simply filling orbitals for the ion's electron count give a wrong transition-metal configuration?

Read an electron transition

Connect discrete energy levels with line emission spectra. These questions concern photons and transitions rather than filling orbitals.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Emit or absorb?

An electron moves from a higher energy level to a lower energy level. What happens?

Answer: A photon is emitted. The atom emits a photon with energy equal to the difference between the two levels.

Compare photons

Transition A has a larger energy gap than transition B. How does the emitted photon from A compare?

Answer: Higher frequency and shorter wavelength. E = hf and c = fλ. Greater photon energy means greater frequency and shorter wavelength; the speed in vacuum is unchanged.

Why lines?

Why does an isolated atomic gas produce discrete spectral lines rather than every possible wavelength?

Answer: Only specific electron energy differences are allowed. Quantized energy levels allow only particular transition energies, producing characteristic wavelengths.

Question 1

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Phosphorus has atomic number 15. Which is its ground-state electron configuration?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p³

Fifteen electrons fill the lower-energy sublevels before giving 3s²3p³ in the outer main energy level.

Question 2

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Which is the electron configuration of Cl⁻, given that chlorine has atomic number 17?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. [Ne]3s²3p⁶

The chloride ion has 18 electrons, one more than the neutral chlorine atom.

Question 3

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Cobalt has atomic number 27 and the neutral configuration [Ar]3d⁷4s². Which is the configuration of Co²⁺?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. [Ar]3d⁷

The two 4s electrons are removed when Co²⁺ forms.

Question 4

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Which is the ground-state electron configuration of copper, atomic number 29?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹

Copper is an exception to the simplest filling pattern and has a filled 3d sublevel with one 4s electron.

Question 5

Skills: Arithmetic and algebra

A neutral atom has configuration [Ne]3s²3p¹. What is its atomic number?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 13

The neon core accounts for 10 electrons, and 3s²3p¹ adds three more. A neutral atom therefore has 13 protons.

Question 6

Skills: Arithmetic and algebra

What is the maximum number of electrons in the third main energy level?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. 18

A main energy level can hold 2n² electrons; with n = 3, this is 18.

Question 7

Skills: Arithmetic and algebra

How many orbitals are present in a p sublevel?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 3

A p sublevel contains three orbitals with different spatial orientations; each can hold two electrons.

Question 8

Skills: Arithmetic and algebra

What is the maximum number of electrons that a d sublevel can contain?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. 10

A d sublevel contains five orbitals, each capable of holding two electrons.

Question 9

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Which statement correctly distinguishes a p orbital from a p sublevel?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. One p orbital holds at most two electrons, while a complete p sublevel holds at most six.

The p sublevel contains three p orbitals, each with a two-electron capacity.

Question 10

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Two electrons occupy the same orbital. Which arrangement is allowed?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. They have opposite spins.

The Pauli exclusion principle requires the two electrons in one orbital to have opposite spins.

Question 11

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Three electrons occupy the three p orbitals of an isolated atom in its ground state. Which arrangement follows Hund's rule?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. One electron in each orbital, with parallel spins

Degenerate orbitals are occupied singly with parallel spins before electrons pair.

Question 12

Skills: Arithmetic and algebra

How many unpaired electrons are present in the 2p sublevel of a ground-state oxygen atom with configuration 1s²2s²2p⁴?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 2

For p⁴, three orbitals first receive one electron each; the fourth pairs in one, leaving two unpaired electrons.

Question 13

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Which feature correctly describes an s atomic orbital?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Its probability distribution is spherically symmetric about the nucleus.

An s orbital is spherically symmetric. An orbital describes an electron probability distribution, not a fixed path.

Question 14

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

What happens when an electron in an isolated hydrogen atom moves from n = 2 to n = 5?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The atom absorbs energy.

Moving to a higher energy level requires absorption of energy.

Question 15

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Which electron transition in hydrogen emits ultraviolet radiation by ending at the first main energy level?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. n = 3 to n = 1

Emission to n = 1 belongs to the ultraviolet region; the upward transition is absorption.

Question 16

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Which transition in hydrogen can produce a visible emission line?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. n = 4 to n = 2

Visible hydrogen emission lines arise from higher levels falling to n = 2.

Question 17

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Blue light and red light travel at the same speed in a vacuum. Which comparison is correct?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Blue light has a higher frequency and higher photon energy than red light.

Blue light has a shorter wavelength, hence a higher frequency and higher photon energy.

Question 18

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Why does a hydrogen discharge tube produce separate emission lines instead of every possible colour?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Only certain differences between electron energy levels are possible.

Discrete allowed electron energies produce photons with specific energies when transitions occur.

Question 19

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

As the main energy level n increases in hydrogen, how does the spacing between adjacent energy levels change?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. The levels become closer together.

Hydrogen energy levels converge at higher energies, so adjacent levels become closer together.

Question 20

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

An electron falls between two energy levels separated by a larger energy gap than in another transition. How does the emitted photon compare?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. It has higher frequency and shorter wavelength.

A larger energy gap gives a higher-energy photon; frequency increases while wavelength decreases.
