R3.1 · Harder task

Dilution before titration

Dilute 10.0 cm³ of an HCl solution to 100.0 cm³. A 25.0 cm³ aliquot of the diluted acid requires 20.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ NaOH for complete neutralization. HCl and NaOH react 1:1.

Part 1

Hint

The NaOH amount fixes the HCl amount by stoichiometry.

Worked solution

n(NaOH)=0.100×0.0200=0.00200 mol, equal to HCl.

Part 2

Hint

First divide by aliquot volume, then undo the tenfold dilution.

Worked solution

Diluted concentration=0.00200/0.0250=0.0800; original=0.800 mol dm⁻³.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

The aliquot contains only one quarter of the flask’s acid. Pair amount with the volume that actually contains it; use either aliquot amount/aliquot volume or four times that amount/full volume.

R3.2 · Harder task

Balance electron transfer

Zinc reacts with Cu²⁺: Zn(s)+Cu²⁺(aq)→Zn²⁺(aq)+Cu(s). Start with 0.0200 mol Zn and 0.0300 mol Cu²⁺. Use M(Cu)=63.5 g mol⁻¹.

Part 1

Hint

Each zinc atom loses two electrons; identify the limiting reactant first.

Worked solution

Zn is limiting in the 1:1 reaction. Electron amount=2×0.0200=0.0400 mol.

Part 2

Hint

Each mole of Zn supplies the electrons for one mole of Cu.

Worked solution

n(Cu)=0.0200 mol; mass=0.0200×63.5=1.27 g.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

Zn→Zn²⁺+2e⁻ and Cu²⁺+2e⁻→Cu. Cu²⁺ accepts electrons and is reduced, so it is the oxidizing agent. The electrons cancel from the overall equation.

R3.3 · Harder task

Audit a radical chain

For chlorination of methane, consider initiation Cl₂→2Cl• and propagation Cl•+CH₄→HCl+CH₃• followed by CH₃•+Cl₂→CH₃Cl+Cl•.

Part 1

Hint

Count chlorine radicals on the right.

Worked solution

One Cl₂ produces two Cl• radicals.

Part 2

Hint

Count radicals entering and leaving the combined steps.

Worked solution

One Cl• is used and one regenerated; CH₃• is an intermediate. Net radical-count change is zero.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

Net: CH₄+Cl₂→CH₃Cl+HCl. A termination example is Cl•+CH₃•→CH₃Cl: two radicals disappear. CH₃Cl still has C–H bonds, so further radical substitution can produce more highly chlorinated products.

R3.4 · Harder task

Track an electron-pair donation

In the model substitution OH⁻+CH₃Br→CH₃OH+Br⁻, the C–Br bond breaks heterolytically as a lone pair on oxygen forms the new C–O bond.

Part 1

Hint

A full curly arrow moves one electron pair.

Worked solution

Two electrons move from the oxygen lone pair toward the electron-deficient carbon.

Part 2

Hint

Methanol is neutral; bromide has charge −1.

Worked solution

0+(−1)=−1, matching the reactant total.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

One arrow begins at an O lone pair and ends at C; the other begins at the C–Br bond and ends at Br. Heterolytic cleavage transfers the whole bonding pair to Br, producing Br⁻. Splitting equally would describe homolysis and radical products instead.
