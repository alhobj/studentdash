Hands-on explorer · SL 1.2

Build a savings sequence

Start with: SL 1.2 small steps

Change the first weekly deposit and the fixed increase. Compare the nth deposit with the total saved. This is an arithmetic sequence with no interest.

Try a fixed scenario

With first deposit 10 and increase 5, what is the total deposited over 10 weeks?

Worked explanation

S₁₀ = 10/2 × [2(10) + 9(5)] = 325. The tenth deposit alone is 55.

Hands-on explorer · SL 1.4

Compare compound and simple growth

Start with: SL 1.4 small steps

Explore a fictional savings account with annual compounding and no fees, tax or additional deposits. Mint is compound growth; gold is simple growth.

Try a fixed scenario

At 5% compounded annually, what is a balance of 1000 worth after 2 years?

Worked explanation

1000 × 1.05² = 1102.50. Apply each year's increase to the updated balance.

Hands-on explorer · SL 1.7

Step through a loan

Start with: SL 1.7 small steps

Use a fictional loan with monthly interest followed by an end-of-month payment. Advance one month at a time. Changing a control starts a new loan; fees are omitted.

Try a fixed scenario

A debt of 5000 accrues 0.5% monthly interest, then a payment of 200 is made. What balance remains?

Worked explanation

5000 × 1.005 − 200 = 4825. The interest charged is 25.

Scale and round a measurement

Adjust a normalized coefficient and an integer power of ten. Compare standard form, decimal value, decimal-place rounding and significant figures.

Try a fixed scenario

Write 0.00072 as a × 10⁻⁴. Find a.

Worked explanation

0.00072 = 7.2 × 10⁻⁴; a normalized positive coefficient is at least 1 and below 10.

Hands-on explorer · SL 1.3

Compare geometric terms and totals

Start with: SL 1.3 small steps

Change the first term and common ratio, including negative ratios. The table is a finite sequence; its sum is not an infinite series.

Try a fixed scenario

Find the sum of the first five terms of 3, 6, 12, … .

Worked explanation

3 + 6 + 12 + 24 + 48 = 93. In sigma notation this is Σ from k=0 to 4 of 3 × 2ᵏ.

Hands-on explorer · SL 1.4

Include inflation and depreciation

Start with: SL 1.4 small steps

Compare nominal savings, purchasing power and a depreciating asset. The annual savings rate is nominal, compounded m times per year; inflation and depreciation are annual effective rates. No fees or taxes.

Try a fixed scenario

An asset worth 1000 loses 10% of its value each year. Find its value after two years.

Worked explanation

1000 × 0.9² = 810. Purchasing power of savings is nominal balance divided by (1 + inflation rate)ᵗ.

Hands-on explorer · SL 1.5

Undo an exponential

Start with: SL 1.5 small steps

Explore exponent laws for integer exponents and logarithms as inverse operations. Select base 10 or e for the graph. The integer-base calculation below uses base 2.

Try a fixed scenario

Solve 10ˣ = 0.001. Find x.

Worked explanation

10⁻³ = 0.001, so log₁₀(0.001) = −3. Logarithms require positive arguments.

Hands-on explorer · SL 1.6

Turn rounding into an interval

Start with: SL 1.6 small steps

Set a measured radius and its rounding unit. Investigate the resulting bounds on circle area and the largest relative discrepancy from the area calculated using the reported radius.

Try a fixed scenario

A length is 8.4 cm to the nearest 0.1 cm. Find its lower bound.

Worked explanation

Half the rounding unit is 0.05: 8.35 ≤ length < 8.45 cm. State bounds before rounding a derived result.

Hands-on explorer · SL 1.7

Build an end-of-period annuity

Start with: SL 1.7 small steps

Deposit a fixed amount at the end of each month. Compare contributions with interest earned. Interest is applied before each new deposit; no fees or taxes.

Try a fixed scenario

Deposit 100 at each year-end with 10% annual interest. What is the balance just after the second deposit?

Worked explanation

The first deposit grows to 110; the second deposit adds 100. Total 210, because the second deposit has not yet earned interest.

Hands-on explorer · SL 1.8 · SL 2.6

Solve a three-variable system

Start with: SL 1.8 small steps · SL 2.6 small steps

Move the three right-hand sides of x + y + z = A, x − y + z = B, and x + y − z = C. A technology-style solver finds a unique solution and displays residuals.

Try a fixed scenario

For x+y+z=6, x−y+z=2 and x+y−z=0, find z.

Worked explanation

Subtract the third equation from the first: 2z = 6, so z = 3. Then y = 2 and x = 1.

Hands-on explorer · SL 1.8 · SL 2.4

Locate polynomial roots and intersections

Start with: SL 1.8 small steps · SL 2.4 small steps

Move q in f(x) = x³ − 3x and the horizontal line g(x) = q. The solver reports all real intersections in the displayed interval, including tangent intersections at q = ±2. Gold is g; mint is f.

Try a fixed scenario

How many distinct real solutions does x³ − 3x = 0 have?

Worked explanation

x(x² − 3) = 0 gives −√3, 0, √3. A plotted crossing is a root of f(x) − q.
