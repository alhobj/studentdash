Method and worked example

Divide each initial amount by its coefficient: n(H₂)/2 and n(O₂)/1. The smaller value sets the reaction extent. Multiply this by 2 to find n(H₂O).

With 4.0 mol H₂ and 3.0 mol O₂, the extent is min(2.0, 3.0) = 2.0 mol. H₂ is limiting; 4.0 mol H₂O forms and 1.0 mol O₂ remains. Equal ratios mean neither reactant is in excess.

Separate yield from atom economy

Use fictional reaction data to distinguish recovered product from how efficiently a balanced reaction directs reactant mass into the desired product.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Percentage yield

The theoretical product mass is 8.00 g and the isolated product mass is 6.00 g. Find the percentage yield.

Answer: 75. Percentage yield = actual / theoretical × 100 = 6.00 / 8.00 × 100 = 75.0%.

Atom economy

In A + B → P + Q, P is the desired product. For the stoichiometric quantities shown, the total reactant mass is 100 g and the theoretical mass of P is 70 g. Find the atom economy.

Answer: 70. Atom economy = theoretical mass of desired product / total stoichiometric reactant mass × 100 = 70%. It is determined by the reaction, not by product recovery.

Interpret both metrics

A reaction has 100% yield but 50% atom economy. What does this mean?

Answer: All theoretical desired product was recovered, but half the stoichiometric reactant mass forms other products. Yield measures recovery relative to a theoretical amount. Atom economy measures how reactant mass is distributed among products.
