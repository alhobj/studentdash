Find the temperature boundary (AHL)

For fictional data use ΔG° = ΔH° − TΔS°. Convert entropy from J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ to kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Assume ΔH° and ΔS° are constant over the temperature range.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

At 300 K

ΔH° = +40.0 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS° = +100 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Calculate ΔG° at 300 K.

Answer: 10. ΔS° = 0.100 kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. ΔG° = 40.0 − 300 × 0.100 = +10.0 kJ mol⁻¹. The forward change is not spontaneous under standard-state conditions at this temperature.

Boundary temperature

With ΔH° = +40.0 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS° = +100 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, at what temperature is ΔG° = 0?

Answer: 400. T = ΔH° / ΔS° = 40.0 / 0.100 = 400 K. Above this temperature ΔG° is negative within this constant-data approximation.

Thermodynamics versus speed

A reaction has negative ΔG under the stated conditions. What can you conclude?

Answer: It is thermodynamically spontaneous but may be slow. The Gibbs energy change describes thermodynamic direction. A large activation barrier can make a spontaneous change very slow.

Account for entropy changes (AHL)

Predict changes in energy and matter dispersal, then use supplied fictional standard molar entropies. Keep entropy units separate from enthalpy units.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Vaporization

What is the usual sign of the system's entropy change when a liquid vaporizes at its boiling point?

Answer: Positive. The gas has many more accessible arrangements than the liquid. The system's entropy increases even though liquid and gas can coexist at equilibrium.

Calculate ΔS°

For A → 2B, S°(A) = 120 and S°(B) = 90 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Calculate ΔS° for the reaction as written.

Answer: 60. ΔS° = 2(90) − 120 = +60 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Entropy changes include the stoichiometric coefficients.

Temperature dependence

A reaction has ΔH < 0 and ΔS > 0. Assuming these signs remain unchanged, when is ΔG = ΔH − TΔS negative?

Answer: At all positive temperatures. The enthalpy term is negative and subtracting positive TΔS makes ΔG still more negative. This predicts thermodynamic favourability, not reaction speed.
