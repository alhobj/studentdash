Sort a radical chain reaction

Classify steps in the photochemical chlorination of methane. A dot denotes an unpaired electron; a radical is not the same as an ion.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Cl₂ → 2Cl•

UV light causes Cl₂ → 2Cl•. Which stage is this?

Answer: Initiation. Homolytic bond fission gives one bonding electron to each chlorine atom, creating two radicals.

Cl• + CH₄ → HCl + CH₃•

A chlorine radical removes a hydrogen atom from methane, producing a methyl radical. Which stage is this?

Answer: Propagation. One radical is consumed and another is formed, allowing the chain to continue. CH₃• can react with Cl₂ to form CH₃Cl and regenerate Cl•.

CH₃• + Cl• → CH₃Cl

A methyl radical combines with a chlorine radical to form chloromethane. Which stage is this?

Answer: Termination. Two radicals combine and no new radical is formed, ending that chain pathway.

Predict radical products and limitations

Apply radical chain ideas to methane chlorination under UV light. Think about atom conservation, side products and why a single pure product is difficult to obtain.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Regenerate the carrier

Complete CH₃• + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + ?

Answer: Cl•. A chlorine radical is regenerated, allowing another propagation step. Both chlorine atoms are accounted for.

Combine methyl radicals

Which product forms when two CH₃• radicals combine in a termination step?

Answer: Ethane, C₂H₆. The two carbon-centred radicals form a C–C bond: CH₃• + •CH₃ → CH₃CH₃. No radical remains.

Further substitution

Why can methane chlorination produce CH₂Cl₂ as well as CH₃Cl?

Answer: The first substitution product can undergo further substitution. CH₃Cl still contains C–H bonds. Further radical substitution can replace additional hydrogen atoms, giving a mixture of products.
