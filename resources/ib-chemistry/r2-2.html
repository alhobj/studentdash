Run and compare a reaction (AHL)

Set the initial concentration and first-order rate constant for a fictional irreversible A → B reaction. Advance the clock to collect a concentration trace. Save one run for comparison.

Your challenge: Compare two initial concentrations with the same k. Do they halve in the same time? Then double k and compare the half-life.

Model, method and limits

This idealized constant-volume model uses [A] = [A]₀exp(−kt), [B] = [A]₀ − [A] and t½ = ln(2)/k. It assumes constant temperature and first-order kinetics, with no reverse reaction. Changing the setup starts a new run.

Read rate evidence

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Average rate

A reactant concentration decreases from 0.80 to 0.50 mol dm⁻³ over 60.0 s. Calculate its positive average rate of disappearance.

Answer: 0.005. Rate of disappearance = −Δ[reactant]/Δt = (0.80 − 0.50)/60.0 = 0.0050 mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹. This is an interval average, not an instantaneous rate.

Catalyst

At the same temperature, what does a catalyst change?

Answer: It provides a pathway with lower activation energy. A catalyst increases forward and reverse rates without changing ΔH or the equilibrium constant at fixed temperature.

Surface area

Equal masses of a solid react with identical excess acid under otherwise identical conditions. Which usually gives the greater initial rate?

Answer: Finely divided powder. The powder exposes more surface for collisions with the acid. Equal mass does not imply equal exposed surface area.

Infer a rate equation (AHL)

Use invented initial-rate comparisons at constant temperature. Change only one reactant concentration at a time; do not infer reaction orders from overall equation coefficients.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Order in A

Doubling [A] while holding all other reactant concentrations fixed makes the initial rate four times larger. Find the order with respect to A.

Answer: 2. 2 to the power m equals 4, so m = 2. The reaction is second order with respect to A.

Order in B

Tripling [B] with all other reactant concentrations fixed leaves the initial rate unchanged. Find the order with respect to B.

Answer: 0. 3 to the power zero equals 1. The measured rate is zero order with respect to B over these conditions.

Rate constant

The measured rate law is rate = k[A]². At [A] = 0.20 mol dm⁻³, rate = 0.012 mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹. Calculate k.

Answer: 0.3. k = 0.012 / (0.20)² = 0.300 dm³ mol⁻¹ s⁻¹. The rate-constant units depend on the overall order.
