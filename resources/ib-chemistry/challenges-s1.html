S1.1 · Harder task

Separate and audit a mixture

A dry 12.0 g mixture contains iron, sand and sodium chloride. A magnet removes 3.0 g iron. After dissolving the salt in water and filtering, the dried sand has mass 4.2 g. Assume no other substances.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Units and conditions

T/K = T/°C + 273.15; 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³

Distinguish STP from SATP.

Phase-change temperatures

Compare measured trends.

Part 1

Hint

Use mass conservation on the dry mixture.

Worked solution

12.0−3.0−4.2=4.8 g NaCl.

Part 2

Hint

Divide recovered salt by the inferred original salt mass.

Worked solution

4.32/4.8 ×100=90%.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

Dissolved ions pass through ordinary filter paper with the water. Evaporation or crystallization can recover salt. Losses can arise from solution left on equipment, spillage, or salt remaining in mother liquor. Wet crystals would instead overestimate recovered dry salt mass.

S1.2 · Harder task

An isotope mixture and its ion

A fictional element has isotopes of exact relative masses 24 and 26. Their weighted mean is 24.6. Its atomic number is 12.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Atomic data

Use relative atomic masses.

Constants

Nₐ ≈ 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹; R ≈ 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹

Match the required precision.

Part 1

Hint

Let its fractional abundance be x: 24(1−x)+26x=24.6.

Worked solution

2x=0.6, so x=0.30 or 30%.

Part 2

Hint

Ion formation changes electrons, not protons.

Worked solution

12 protons and charge +2 imply 12−2=10 electrons.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

There are 26−12=14 neutrons. Isotopes have the same proton number, so changing their proportions changes the mean relative atomic mass but not elemental identity.

S1.3 · Harder task

Ion formation and a photon

A neutral magnesium atom has Z=12 and configuration 1s²2s²2p⁶3s². In a separate model, an electron drops between energy levels separated by 4.00 ×10⁻¹⁹ J. Use h=6.626×10⁻³⁴ J s and c=3.00×10⁸ m s⁻¹.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Radiation regions

Compare frequency and wavelength.

Constants

Nₐ ≈ 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹; R ≈ 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹

Match the required precision.

Ionization and electronegativity

Select the correct property.

Part 1

Hint

Remove the two highest-energy outer electrons.

Worked solution

Mg²⁺ is 1s²2s²2p⁶, occupying shells n=1 and n=2.

Part 2

Hint

Use ΔE=hν=hc/λ, then convert metres to nm.

Worked solution

λ=4.9695×10⁻⁷ m=496.95 nm.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

Only particular energy differences between quantized levels are allowed. A photon carries the released energy; a line in a spectrum identifies its wavelength, not the spatial trajectory of the electron.

Purity followed by dilution

A 2.50 g sample is 80.0% by mass NaOH, with inert impurities. Use M(NaOH)=40.0 g mol⁻¹. Dissolve it and make up to 250.0 cm³. Transfer 25.0 cm³ of this solution and dilute to 100.0 cm³.

Practise: Use masses from a table

Part 1

Hint

Find pure NaOH mass, then moles, then divide by 0.2500 dm³.

Worked solution

Pure mass=2.00 g; n=0.0500 mol; c=0.200 mol dm⁻³.

Part 2

Hint

Use c₁V₁=c₂V₂ with consistent volume units.

Worked solution

c₂=0.200×25.0/100.0=0.0500 mol dm⁻³.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

The aliquot contains 0.200×0.0250=0.00500 mol. Adding water increases volume without adding or removing NaOH, so 0.00500 mol remains in the final 0.1000 dm³.

S1.5 · Harder task

Find a gas molar mass

A dry gas sample of mass 0.580 g occupies 0.240 dm³ at 100.0 kPa and 300.0 K. Assume ideal behaviour. Use R=8.314 kPa dm³ mol⁻¹ K⁻¹.

Gas calculations

PV = nRT

Use kelvin. With R = 8.31, pair Pa with m³ or kPa with dm³; use any different R value supplied in the question.

Constants

Nₐ ≈ 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹; R ≈ 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹

Match the required precision.

Units and conditions

T/K = T/°C + 273.15; 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³

Distinguish STP from SATP.

Practise: Match gas-law units

Part 1

Hint

Use n=PV/RT with the supplied units.

Worked solution

n=(100.0×0.240)/(8.314×300.0)≈0.00962232 mol.

Part 2

Hint

Divide sample mass by its unrounded amount.

Worked solution

M=0.580/n≈60.2765 g mol⁻¹.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

Ideal pressure doubles to 200 kPa. At higher density, finite molecular size and intermolecular attractions can become significant compared with the ideal assumptions.
