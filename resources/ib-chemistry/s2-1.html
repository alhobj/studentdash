Build an ionic formula

Add and remove ions to make a neutral formula unit. Then reduce it to the smallest whole-number ratio.

Your challenge: Build aluminium oxide with zero net charge and the smallest possible number of ions. Can a neutral collection still use a ratio that is not simplest?

Model, method and limits

Total charge is the sum of each ion count multiplied by its signed charge. Al³⁺ and O²⁻ balance at two aluminium ions and three oxide ions. The counters represent a formula ratio, not isolated molecules in an ionic solid.

Connect particles to properties

Choose a substance and state, then predict whether it conducts electricity. Use the particles and their mobility to explain your answer.

Schematic only, not to scale. Positions are illustrative; the diagram does not simulate motion.

Compare the three bonding models
  • Ionic: electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions throughout a lattice. Solid NaCl has fixed ions; molten NaCl has mobile ions.
  • Covalent: electrostatic attraction between a shared pair of electrons and both bonded nuclei. Water contains molecules; diamond and graphite form extended covalent structures.
  • Metallic: electrostatic attraction between positive metal ions and delocalized electrons. Mobile electrons allow conduction in solid and molten copper.

Melting ice overcomes intermolecular attractions; it does not break the O–H covalent bonds within each water molecule. Graphite conducts along its layers because some electrons are delocalized. Diamond has no comparable mobile charge carriers. Pure water has very low conductivity due to its slight self-ionization.

Explain: Why is “all covalent substances have low melting points and cannot conduct” incorrect? Use diamond and graphite as counterexamples.

Build a neutral ionic formula

Balance total positive and negative charge using the simplest whole-number ratio. Keep polyatomic ions together.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Magnesium chloride

Which formula combines Mg²⁺ and Cl⁻ in a neutral compound?

Answer: MgCl₂. One Mg²⁺ requires two Cl⁻ ions: +2 + 2(−1) = 0.

Aluminium oxide

Which formula combines Al³⁺ and O²⁻ in the simplest neutral ratio?

Answer: Al₂O₃. Two aluminium ions give +6 and three oxide ions give −6. The simplest ratio is 2:3.

Calcium nitrate

Which formula combines Ca²⁺ with nitrate, NO₃⁻?

Answer: Ca(NO₃)₂. Two nitrate ions balance one calcium ion. Parentheses show that the entire NO₃ group occurs twice.

20 multiple-choice questions

Question 1

Which formula results when Li⁺ and O²⁻ form an electrically neutral ionic compound?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Li₂O

Two Li⁺ ions are required to balance the charge of one O²⁻ ion.

Question 2

What is the formula of the ionic compound formed from Al³⁺ and O²⁻?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Al₂O₃

Two aluminium ions provide +6 and three oxide ions provide −6.

Question 3

What is the formula of magnesium hydroxide?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Mg(OH)₂

Mg²⁺ requires two hydroxide ions, each with charge −1.

Question 4

Which is the formula of calcium nitrate?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Ca(NO₃)₂

Calcium is Ca²⁺ and nitrate is NO₃⁻, so two nitrate ions are needed.

Question 5

What is the formula of ammonium sulfate?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. (NH₄)₂SO₄

Two NH₄⁺ ions balance one SO₄²⁻ ion.

Question 6

Which name correctly describes Cu₂O?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Copper(I) oxide

The oxide ion has charge −2, so each of the two copper ions must have charge +1.

Question 7

What is the formula of iron(III) chloride?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. FeCl₃

Iron(III) is Fe³⁺; three chloride ions are needed per iron ion.

Question 8

Which is the correct name for NaHCO₃?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Sodium hydrogencarbonate

The anion HCO₃⁻ is hydrogencarbonate; carbonate is CO₃²⁻.

Question 9

A main-group atom has outer-shell configuration 3s²3p⁵. Which ion does it normally form to achieve a noble-gas configuration?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. A 1− ion by gaining one electron

Seven outer-shell electrons become eight when one electron is gained, giving charge −1.

Question 10

Why does losing two electrons from a neutral magnesium atom produce a positive ion?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The ion has two more protons than electrons.

The nucleus is unchanged; removing negatively charged electrons leaves a net positive charge.

Question 11

Which statement describes the ionic bond in sodium fluoride?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions

Ionic bonding is the electrostatic attraction between cations and anions.

Question 12

Why does molten sodium chloride conduct electricity while solid sodium chloride does not conduct appreciably?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Its ions can move through the liquid but are fixed in the solid lattice.

Mobile ions transport charge in the melt; the solid contains ions but they cannot move through the lattice.

Question 13

A substance has a high melting point, does not conduct electricity as a solid, and conducts when molten. Which structure best explains these observations?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. An ionic lattice

The observations are characteristic of an ionic solid whose ions become mobile on melting.

Question 14

What does the formula CaF₂ represent in a crystal of calcium fluoride?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. A 1:2 ratio of calcium ions to fluoride ions in an extended lattice

An ionic formula expresses the simplest electrically neutral ratio of ions, not discrete molecules.

Question 15

Two ionic solids have ions of the same charge but different ionic radii. Which change generally strengthens the electrostatic attraction?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. Decreasing the distance between the ion centres

For fixed charges, electrostatic attraction increases as the separation decreases.

Question 16

Two hypothetical ionic lattices have similar distances between neighbouring ions. Lattice X contains 1+ and 1− ions; lattice Y contains 2+ and 2− ions. Which has stronger attraction between neighbouring unlike ions?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Y, because the magnitudes of the ionic charges are larger

At similar separations, larger ionic charge magnitudes give stronger electrostatic attraction.

Question 17

Why do many ionic compounds have low volatility?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Substantial energy is needed to overcome strong attractions throughout the lattice.

Strong lattice attractions make it difficult for particles to escape into the gas phase.

Question 18

Which statement about ionic compounds is correct?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Their solubility in water varies; not all ionic compounds dissolve readily.

An ionic structure does not guarantee high water solubility; properties depend on the particular compound.

Question 19

Which substance contains covalent bonds within a polyatomic ion and ionic attractions between ions?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. NH₄Cl

Ammonium chloride has covalent N–H bonds within NH₄⁺ and ionic attractions between NH₄⁺ and Cl⁻.

Question 20

An ionic compound contains M³⁺ and sulfate ions, SO₄²⁻. What is its empirical formula?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. M₂(SO₄)₃

Two M³⁺ ions and three sulfate ions give charges of +6 and −6.
