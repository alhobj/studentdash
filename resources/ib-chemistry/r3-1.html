Operate a virtual burette

Add 0.100 mol dm⁻³ NaOH to 20.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ HCl. Choose coarse or fine additions and watch the pH curve develop. Undo an addition if you overshoot.

Your challenge: Approach equivalence with large additions, then use small additions to finish within 0.1 cm³. Explain the steep pH change near equivalence.

Model, method and limits

HCl and NaOH react 1:1. The model assumes ideal dilute solutions at 25 °C, additive volumes and Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴. Excess acid or base and water autoionization determine pH. Equivalence occurs at 20.0 cm³ added base. The colour is a pH scale, not a real indicator.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Identify the base

NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻. Which reactant accepts a proton?

Answer: NH₃. NH₃ accepts H⁺ to form NH₄⁺ and acts as a base. Water donates H⁺ in this reaction and acts as an acid.

Conjugate pair

Which pair differs by exactly one proton?

Answer: NH₄⁺ and NH₃. A conjugate acid–base pair differs by one H⁺. The other pairs differ by two protons.

Calculate pH

An ideal dilute solution has [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³. Use pH = −log₁₀[H⁺].

Answer: 3. −log₁₀(0.00100) = 3.00. A tenfold decrease in [H⁺] would increase pH by one.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Convert volume

A titre is 25.0 cm³. Express it in dm³ before using n = cV.

Answer: 0.025. 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³, so 25.0 / 1000 = 0.0250 dm³.

Find concentration

20.0 cm³ of HCl is neutralized by 25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ NaOH. Using HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O, find the HCl concentration.

Answer: 0.125. n(NaOH) = 0.100 × 0.0250 = 0.00250 mol. The ratio is 1:1, so c(HCl) = 0.00250 / 0.0200 = 0.125 mol dm⁻³.

Two protons

For complete neutralization, H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O. How many moles of NaOH are required for 0.0030 mol H₂SO₄?

Answer: 0.006. The mole ratio H₂SO₄:NaOH is 1:2, so 2 × 0.0030 = 0.0060 mol NaOH is required.
