Scenarios and worked explanations

One type of molecule

A sample contains only identical molecules, each made from two atoms of X bonded to one atom of Y. X and Y are different elements. How is the sample classified?

Answer: A pure compound. There is one chemical substance containing two elements in a fixed ratio. Multiple elements in each molecule do not make the sample a mixture.

Recover the solvent

A non-volatile solid is dissolved in water. Which method collects the water as a liquid?

Answer: Simple distillation. The water vaporizes and is condensed into a separate container. Filtration does not remove a dissolved solute; evaporation to dryness does not collect the solvent.

Temperature scale

A fictional sample is at 26.85 °C. Convert this temperature to kelvin using T / K = temperature / °C + 273.15.

Answer: 300. 26.85 + 273.15 = 300.00 K. Temperature in kelvin is proportional to the average kinetic energy in the ideal-gas model.

Melting plateau

A pure solid melts at constant pressure. During the melting plateau, what happens as energy is supplied?

Answer: Average kinetic energy stays approximately constant while potential energy increases. Energy overcomes attractions as the solid becomes liquid. The temperature remains constant during the phase change; particles continue moving.

Choose a separation route

Choose a method from the physical properties of the components. Each scenario has a different substance to recover.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Sand and salt

Sand and salt are mixed. Water dissolves the salt but not the sand. After adding water and stirring, how should you collect the sand?

Answer: Filter the mixture. Sand remains as the residue. Dissolved salt passes through the filter with the water.

Recover salt

After filtering off sand, how can you recover solid salt from the filtrate if collecting the water is unnecessary?

Answer: Evaporate water and crystallize the salt. Removing solvent allows the dissolved salt to crystallize. Dissolved ions pass through an ordinary filter.

Two miscible liquids

Two miscible liquids have different but relatively close boiling points. Which method is best suited to separating them?

Answer: Fractional distillation. Repeated vaporization and condensation in a fractionating column improves separation. A separating funnel is used for immiscible liquid layers.

Question 1

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

A sealed flask contains only neon gas and nitrogen gas. How should its contents be classified?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. A homogeneous mixture of two elements

Neon and nitrogen are elements. The gases mix uniformly without becoming chemically bonded to one another.

Question 2

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Which observation most strongly supports the conclusion that a sample is a mixture rather than a pure compound?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. Different batches can contain different proportions of its components.

Mixtures can have variable composition; a particular pure compound has a fixed ratio of its constituent elements.

Question 3

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

A sample consists entirely of identical molecules, each containing one sulfur atom and two oxygen atoms. What is the sample?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. A pure compound

Every particle has the same fixed combination of different elements, so the sample is a pure compound.

Question 4

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Liquid bromine contains only Br₂ molecules. Which classification is correct?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. An element, because all atoms have the same atomic number

An element can exist as diatomic molecules. The number of atoms per molecule does not determine whether it is a compound.

Question 5

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

A beaker contains liquid water with floating ice, and no other substances. How is this sample best described?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. A pure substance present in two physical states

Both phases consist of H₂O. More than one physical state does not by itself make a sample a mixture.

Question 6

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Which statement distinguishes a compound from a mixture?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Separating a compound into its constituent elements requires chemical change.

A compound contains chemically bonded elements; the components of a mixture can be separated by physical methods.

Question 7

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

A clear solution of sodium chloride is sampled from its top and bottom after thorough mixing. What should be expected?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Both samples have the same composition, provided no evaporation occurs.

A homogeneous mixture is uniform in composition on the scale of ordinary sampling.

Question 8

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

A mixture contains insoluble sand and aqueous sodium chloride. Which sequence can recover dry sand and solid sodium chloride separately?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Filter, then evaporate water from the filtrate

Filtration separates sand from the salt solution. Evaporating the filtrate leaves the dissolved salt.

Question 9

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

During filtration of a suspension, what is the filtrate?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The liquid, including dissolved substances, that passes through the filter

Filtration removes particles of insoluble material; dissolved substances generally pass through with the solvent.

Question 10

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Why does filtering a sodium chloride solution through ordinary filter paper fail to produce pure water?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. The dissolved ions pass through the filter with the water.

Ordinary filtration does not separate a dissolved solute from its solvent.

Question 11

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

A student wants to recover pure water from a solution of a non-volatile salt. Which process is appropriate?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Simple distillation with collection of the condensate

Water vaporizes and can be condensed separately while the non-volatile salt remains in the flask.

Question 12

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

A solid is very soluble in hot water but only slightly soluble in cold water. Which method is suitable for obtaining purified crystals from a crude sample, assuming the impurities remain dissolved on cooling?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Recrystallization from hot water

Dissolving in hot solvent and cooling allows the desired solid to crystallize while the specified impurities remain in solution.

Question 13

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Two miscible liquids have boiling points of 65 °C and 82 °C. What normally becomes enriched in the first fraction collected during fractional distillation?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. The liquid with the lower boiling point

The more volatile component contributes a larger proportion of the vapour and is enriched in the early distillate.

Question 14

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Dry ice changes directly from solid carbon dioxide into gas. What is this change called?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: C. Sublimation

Sublimation is the direct change from solid to gas.

Question 15

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Water droplets form on the outside of a cold glass when water vapour in the surrounding air changes state. Which change occurs?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Condensation

Water changes from gas to liquid, which is condensation.

Question 16

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

Which description applies to the particles in a liquid?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: A. They remain close together but can move past one another.

Liquid particles are close together and mobile, allowing a liquid to flow while retaining a nearly fixed volume.

Question 17

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

A pure solid is heated at constant pressure and melts at its melting point. Why can its temperature remain constant while energy is supplied?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. Energy changes particle interactions rather than increasing average kinetic energy during melting.

During a phase change at constant temperature, the energy supplied does not increase the particles' average kinetic energy.

Question 18

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

A laboratory is at 27 °C. What is its temperature in kelvin, to the nearest kelvin?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 300 K

T/K = temperature/°C + 273.15, so 27 °C is 300.15 K, approximately 300 K.

Question 19

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

A sample warms from 15 °C to 33 °C. What is its temperature increase in kelvin?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 18 K

Kelvin and Celsius intervals have the same size: 33 − 15 = 18 °C corresponds to 18 K.

Question 20

Skills: Scientific knowledge and terminology · Scientific models and limits

A gas is heated from 250 K to 500 K. What happens to the average kinetic energy of its particles?

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. It doubles.

Average kinetic energy is proportional to absolute temperature; 500/250 = 2.
