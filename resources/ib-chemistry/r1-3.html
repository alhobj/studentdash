Compare useful energy from fuels

Use supplied fictional fuel data to compare energy per mass and useful output. Keep energy released as a positive quantity here, unlike a negative combustion enthalpy.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Energy per gram

Fuel X has molar mass 40.0 g mol⁻¹ and releases 1600 kJ mol⁻¹ on complete combustion. How much energy is released per gram?

Answer: 40. 1600 / 40.0 = 40.0 kJ g⁻¹. Energy per mole alone is not enough to compare equal masses of fuels.

Useful energy

Burning 2.00 g of fuel X releases 40.0 kJ g⁻¹. A device transfers 30.0% of this energy to its intended use. Calculate the useful energy.

Answer: 24. Total energy = 2.00 × 40.0 = 80.0 kJ. Useful energy = 80.0 × 0.300 = 24.0 kJ.

Limited oxygen

Why can burning a hydrocarbon with insufficient oxygen be dangerous?

Answer: It can form toxic carbon monoxide. Incomplete combustion may form carbon monoxide and soot rather than converting all carbon to carbon dioxide.

Compare a fuel cell with combustion

Consider hydrogen as an energy carrier and distinguish what happens inside a device from the environmental effects of producing its fuel.

Choose a scenario, enter a prediction and check it. Try again or reveal the explanation, then move to another scenario.

Scenarios and worked explanations

Energy conversion

What is the main useful energy conversion in a hydrogen–oxygen fuel cell?

Answer: Chemical energy directly to electrical energy. Separated electrode reactions transfer electrons through an external circuit. Heat is also produced; the cell is not perfectly efficient.

Overall product

In the ideal overall reaction 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, how many moles of water form from 0.60 mol H₂ with excess O₂?

Answer: 0.6. The H₂:H₂O ratio is 1:1, so 0.60 mol hydrogen forms 0.60 mol water.

Production matters

A fuel cell produces water during use. Does that alone establish that its hydrogen has zero associated carbon emissions?

Answer: No, the hydrogen production and energy sources also matter. Hydrogen can be produced through different processes. Assessing environmental impact requires considering production and energy supply as well as use.

Question 1

Skills: Arithmetic and algebra

A fuel releases 40 kJ g⁻¹. Find energy in kJ from complete combustion of 10 g. Choose the closest value.

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: B. 400

Multiply specific energy by mass burned. The result is 400.

Question 2

Skills: Arithmetic and algebra

A heater receives 200 kJ of fuel energy and delivers 140 kJ usefully. Find efficiency as a percentage. Choose the closest value.

Show the correct answer and explanation

Correct answer: D. 70

Efficiency = useful output/input × 100%. The result is 70.

Skills: Arithmetic and algebra

Show the correct answer and explanation
