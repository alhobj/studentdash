R1.1 · Harder task

Calorimetry with a calorimeter correction

A reaction warms 100.0 g of solution from 20.0 to 26.0 °C. Use c=4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹ and calorimeter heat capacity 50.0 J K⁻¹. The reaction consumes 0.0500 mol of limiting reactant. Ignore heat loss.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Calorimetry

Q = mcΔT

Use the mass of the heated material. Convert J to kJ when needed; for an insulated setup the reaction and solution have opposite heat signs.

Constants

Nₐ ≈ 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹; R ≈ 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹

Match the required precision.

Uncertainty propagation

Choose the matching operation.

Practise: Track the direction of heat transfer

Part 1

Skills: Arithmetic and algebra

Hint

Both solution and apparatus warm by 6.0 K.

Worked solution

q=(100.0×4.18+50.0)×6.0=2808 J.

Part 2

Skills: Arithmetic and algebra

Hint

The reaction loses the heat gained by its surroundings.

Worked solution

ΔH=−2.808/0.0500=−56.16 kJ mol⁻¹.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

Ignoring the calorimeter omits absorbed heat and gives a less negative result. Heat loss lowers the observed temperature rise and also makes the calculated exothermic enthalpy less negative, under the same simplifying assumptions.

Build a Hess cycle

Use C(s)+O₂(g)→CO₂(g), ΔH=−394 kJ mol⁻¹, and CO(g)+½O₂(g)→CO₂(g), ΔH=−283 kJ mol⁻¹.

Hint

Reverse the second equation, then add and cancel CO₂.

Worked solution

−394+283=−111 kJ mol⁻¹.

Part 2

Skills: Arithmetic and algebra

Hint

Scale the equation and energy together.

Worked solution

0.75×(−111)=−83.25 kJ.

Reversal swaps initial and final states, reversing the energy difference. A catalyst changes the pathway and activation energy without changing reactant and product enthalpies.

Compare fuels by energy and emissions

Complete combustion of CH₄ releases 890 kJ mol⁻¹ and produces 1 mol CO₂ per mol fuel. H₂ releases 286 kJ mol⁻¹ and produces no CO₂ at use. Use M(CH₄)=16.0, M(H₂)=2.00, M(CO₂)=44.0 g mol⁻¹.

R1.4 · AHL · Harder task

A temperature threshold (AHL)

For a fictional process, ΔH=+40.0 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS=+120 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. Treat both as constant over the interval.

Booklet help: formulas and data

Related references for this section. Use values and assumptions stated in the question.

Gibbs energy (AHL)

ΔG° = ΔH° − TΔS°

Convert entropy and enthalpy to matching energy units; use kelvin. A negative result indicates thermodynamic feasibility under standard conditions.

Thermodynamic values

Match physical states. Enthalpy values use kJ mol⁻¹, while entropy uses J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.

Part 1

Skills: Arithmetic and algebra

Hint

Convert entropy units before using T=ΔH/ΔS.

Worked solution

ΔS=0.120 kJ mol⁻¹ K⁻¹, so T=40.0/0.120≈333.333 K.

Part 2

Skills: Arithmetic and algebra

Hint

Use ΔG=ΔH−TΔS.

Worked solution

40.0−400×0.120=−8.00 kJ mol⁻¹.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

With positive ΔS, ΔG becomes negative above the threshold in this approximation. A large activation barrier can still produce a slow reaction. The constant ΔH and ΔS assumption can fail over wide temperature ranges.
