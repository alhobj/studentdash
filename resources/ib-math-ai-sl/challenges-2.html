2.1 · Harder task

Two tariffs and a perpendicular

Tariff A costs y=3x+12. Tariff B passes through (2,24) and (6,40), where x is hours and y is cost.

Part 1

Hint

Find B’s slope and intercept, then equate the two lines.

Worked solution

B has slope 4 and intercept 16. 3x+12=4x+16 gives x=−4. No equal cost occurs at non-negative hours.

Part 2

Hint

Use the negative reciprocal of B’s nonzero slope.

Worked solution

Perpendicular slope = −1/4.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

B−A = x+4 > 0 for x≥0, so A is always cheaper in the physical domain. The algebraic intersection lies outside it.

2.2 · Harder task

Invert a restricted model

A pricing model is f(x)=5−2x on −1≤x≤3.

Part 1

Hint

The slope is negative; inspect the right endpoint.

Worked solution

f(3)=−1 and f(−1)=7, so the range is [−1,7].

Part 2

Hint

Solve 6=5−2x, or use x=(5−y)/2.

Worked solution

f⁻¹(6)=(5−6)/2=−0.5.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

The inverse domain is [−1,7], the original range. Although the rearranged expression can take 8, that value was never an output of the restricted original function.

2.3 · Harder task

Combine a revenue and cost graph

Revenue R(x)=−x²+12x and cost C(x)=2x+9, for 0≤x≤10. Profit is R−C.

Part 1

Hint

Complete the square in −x²+10x−9.

Worked solution

P(x)=−(x−5)²+16, so the maximum is 16.

Part 2

Hint

Set the profit to zero.

Worked solution

x²−10x+9=0 gives x=1 or 9.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

Profit is positive between the roots, zero at 1 and 9, and negative for 0≤x<1 and 9<x≤10. Subtract graph heights in the correct order.

2.4 · Harder task

Choose a graph window

Consider f(x)=6/(x−2)+1.

Part 1

Hint

Set the whole function to zero and solve.

Worked solution

6/(x−2)=−1 gives x=−4.

Part 2

Hint

Isolate the reciprocal term.

Worked solution

6/(x−2)=3 gives x=4.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

The asymptotes are x=2 and y=1. x=2 is outside the domain; joining samples across it draws a false part of the curve.

2.5 · Harder task

Fit a seasonal model

A seasonal quantity has maximum 18 and minimum 6. Its period is 12 months and a maximum occurs at t=0. Use y=a cos(bt)+d with degrees.

Part 1

Hint

Amplitude is half the max–min difference.

Worked solution

a=(18−6)/2=6; the principal axis is d=12.

Part 2

Hint

Use period = 360/b.

Worked solution

b=360/12=30; y=6cos(30t)+12.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

At t=3, cos90°=0, so y=12. Long-term changes can alter the mean, amplitude or period; a periodic fit does not establish permanent repetition.

2.6 · Harder task

Validate a model

Fit y=ax²+bx+c through (0,2), (1,5), (2,12). A reserved observation at x=3 has y=22.

Part 1

Hint

Use c=2, then solve a+b=3 and 4a+2b=10.

Worked solution

Subtract twice the first equation: 2a=4, so a=2 and b=1.

Part 2

Hint

Predict at x=3 without refitting.

Worked solution

Prediction = 2×9+3+2=23; residual=22−23=−1.

Explain and evaluate

Compare your reasoning

An exact fit to training points is guaranteed by the three-parameter construction. A separate observation tests prediction and can reveal a poor model family or measurement variation.
